## Design Context

### Users
Recruiters, potential employers, and former colleagues — people who encounter this site after receiving a referral, finding a CV, or seeing James mentioned somewhere. They are visiting during a professional evaluation moment: assessing whether he's worth a conversation. Context: likely desktop, during work hours, briefly scanning before deciding to read more deeply. The job to be done is: "Is this person credible, interesting, and worth reaching out to?"

### Brand Personality
**3 words: exact, human, ahead.**

Not "modern", not "elegant" — those are dead categories. Exact: his writing is precise and unsentimental, no wasted words. Human: he leads with empathy and people, not credentials and achievements. Ahead: the narrative is explicitly about the future, about agentic AI, about what's coming next, not a retrospective résumé.

Emotional goal: a visitor should leave with the feeling "this person brings something different — and I want to talk to them." Not impressed, not dazzled — *curious* and *convinced*.

Tone: quietly confident. No exclamation points. No superlatives. No buzzwords. The writing already does this; the design must too.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Dark, editorial, asymmetric.** Not dark-mode-as-trend — dark because it signals focus, precision, and a certain seriousness. The palette is already defined and good: navy/slate backgrounds with a warm amber accent. That pairing is unusual and memorable — most dark portfolios reach for cyan or purple. Amber signals warmth without softness.

**Primary reference: Carlos Mendoza portfolio** — strong asymmetric layouts, bold display type, yellow/amber accents used sparingly, photography of the person featured prominently, stats displayed as moments rather than a grid of metrics, clear section-to-section rhythm without monotony.

**Anti-reference:** Generic dark developer portfolios — cyan-on-dark, purple gradient backgrounds, same left-column-icons-right-column-text layout. Any site that could be described as "looks like it was made from a template."

**Theme:** Dark. Already decided and right — a precise, focused professional working at the frontier uses a tool that feels focused and precise.

**Typography pairing:** James is open to introducing a second typeface. League Spartan (the primary font, already implemented) is a bold, tightly-tracked geometric sans — it works well for headings, eyebrows, and labels. A contrasting editorial serif for body text paragraphs would add depth, reinforce the "craftsmanship" value, and create the kind of typographic hierarchy that makes a site feel designed rather than coded. Avoid anything in the reflex-banned list. Look at: Gloock, Libre Caslon Display, Iowan Old Style (web), Reforma, or similar editorial-weight serifs.

### Design Principles

1. **Earn every element.** No decorative flourish, no section exists unless it does a specific job. If something could be removed without loss, remove it. James's writing already does this — the design must match.

2. **Asymmetry over symmetry.** Centred layouts feel safe and forgettable. Left-aligned text, offset compositions, varied section structures, unexpected use of whitespace — these make a site feel considered.

3. **The accent is a signal, not a fill.** The amber/gold accent (oklch(80% 0.158 75)) should appear rarely: for emphasis, for the one thing per section that matters most. When everything glows amber, nothing glows amber.

4. **Deliberate, editorial motion.** A few well-placed, well-orchestrated moments — page entrance, scroll reveals, hover states — executed with restraint. Fast transitions (not bouncy). The sense that motion was *decided*, not applied as a default.

5. **Show the person, not the CV.** James's values — craftsmanship, empathy, originality — should be physically felt in the design choices, not just stated in the copy. The site IS an expression of how he thinks.
