# Impeccable Design Context — jamesmaggs.com

## Design Context

### Users

Sharp technical peers — engineers, leaders, and builders who are serious about where software engineering is going. They arrive via referral, LinkedIn, or a CV. They are evaluating whether James is someone worth knowing and thinking alongside, not just someone with the right skills.

Secondary: recruiters and potential employers. The site should work for them without being optimised for them first.

Context: likely desktop, during work hours. Briefly scanning before deciding to read more deeply. The job to be done: "Is this person credible, interesting, and worth a conversation?"

### Brand Personality

**3 words: exact, human, ahead.**

- **Exact**: writing is precise and unsentimental. No wasted words. No hedging.
- **Human**: leads with empathy and people, not credentials and achievements.
- **Ahead**: the narrative is about the future, about agentic AI, about what's coming next.

Emotional goal: a visitor should leave thinking *"this person has seen it all, knows what's real, and I want to talk to them."* Not impressed, not dazzled — *curious* and *convinced*.

Tone: quietly confident. No exclamation points. No superlatives. No buzzwords.

The site is a long-horizon professional presence, not a hire-me page. The contact CTA filters for peers: *"If you're thinking about what's next too, let's talk."*

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dark, editorial, asymmetric.**

- **Dark** because it signals focus, precision, and seriousness.
- **Editorial** because the content is narrative and opinionated, not transactional.
- **Asymmetric** because symmetry implies safety; this site should feel decided.

**Palette:** Navy/slate backgrounds with warm amber accent `oklch(80% 0.158 75)`. The amber is the single memorable accent — unusual, warm, used sparingly. Not cyan. Not purple. Not teal.

**Typography is the signature.** The user has confirmed: typography and spacing are the most important visual elements — the thing someone should remember. Every typographic decision should be deliberate. Hierarchy through scale and weight, not through decoration.

**Typography pairing (established):**
- **League Spartan** (geometric sans-serif, 500–800 weight): all display, headings, labels, UI, navigation.
- **Bitter** (screen-optimised serif, 400 regular + italic): all body paragraphs, running text, pull quotes.

These fonts are already loaded and in use. Do not replace them.

**Primary reference:** Carlos Mendoza — strong asymmetric layouts, bold display type, amber/yellow accents used sparingly, clear section rhythm without monotony.

**Anti-reference:** Generic dark developer portfolios — cyan-on-dark, purple gradients, icon-left-text-right template layouts, glowing borders, identical card grids.

### Design Principles

1. **Typography and spacing carry the design.** These are the site's visual signature. Every sizing, weight, tracking, and spacing decision should be deliberate and precise. When in doubt, add space above rather than decoration around.

2. **Earn every element.** No decorative flourish unless it does a specific job. If removing an element makes the page cleaner without losing meaning, remove it.

3. **Asymmetry over symmetry.** Left-aligned, offset compositions, varied section structures. Sections should look different from each other in layout while feeling coherent in tone.

4. **The accent is a signal, not a fill.** Amber appears rarely — for the one thing per section that matters most. Overuse kills the signal.

5. **Show the person, not the CV.** Craftsmanship, empathy, and originality should be felt in the design choices, not just stated in the copy.

### Motion

Deliberate and restrained. The site currently uses scroll reveals (IntersectionObserver, fade-up + translate). That's the right level. No decorative animation. Motion should convey state, not entertain.

### Future Direction

A writing section (short notes, essays, thinking-in-public about agentic engineering) is a planned future addition. Preferred path: Eleventy as static site generator. The delivered site remains static HTML. Do not introduce build tooling until writing is actually being published.

When the writing section is designed, it should maintain the same typographic and spacing rigour. The editorial feel of the current site is the right template for a writing/notes section.
