## Design Context

### Users
Primary audience is sharp technical peers — engineers, engineering leaders, builders who can tell craft from theatre. Recruiters are a distant secondary audience. Visitors arrive having heard of James (often via LinkedIn or a referral) and the page has to answer one question: *"Is this person credible, interesting, and worth a conversation?"* They scan first, then read selectively. A typical session is a few minutes on desktop, often during a working day. The contact action is not a transaction — it's the start of a conversation.

### Brand Personality
Quietly authoritative *and* warm. Two strands held together:

- **Considered, craftsmanlike, dry** — has been around for thirty years, doesn't need to perform, evidence over assertion, wry over enthusiastic.
- **Warm, curious, generous** — genuinely interested in passing on what he's learned, wants conversations not transactions, approachable without being effusive.

Emotional response on first encounter: *"this person is thoughtful and has interesting things to say."* On exit: *"I want to talk to them."*

Voice rules (already enforced in copy):
- No exclamation points.
- No superlatives ("best", "leading", "most").
- No buzzwords or marketing-speak ("leverage", "unlock", "transform").
- No urgency or scarcity language.

### Aesthetic Direction
Sits in the **personal-craft site** territory — closer to an individual writer/engineer's site or a studio/typographer's catalog than to a corporate tech brand or an editorial magazine. Designed with care, individual voice, considered details that reward attention without announcing themselves.

- **Theme**: dark. Slate-blue surfaces, warm amber as a single signal accent used sparingly (one element per section at most).
- **Typography**: League Spartan (display, 500–700) paired with Bitter (serif, 400 + italic) for body and pull quotes. No other typefaces.
- **Layout**: asymmetric, left-aligned, varied per section. The page has rhythm — no repeating templates, no identical card grids.

Anti-references — what this is **not**:
- **Solopreneur funnel** (the explicit anti-pick). No testimonial walls, no "Book a call" CTA blocks, no "as seen in" logo strips, no urgency banners, no popups, no over-eager conversion patterns.
- Generic agency template (gradient hero, identical card grids).
- Corporate consultancy aesthetic (stock photos, hero metrics, formal tone).
- AI-default look (cyan-on-dark, purple-to-blue gradients, neon accents, glassmorphism).

### Design Principles

1. **Restraint is the authority signal.** Amber appears sparingly *because* it works as a signal — one element per section at most. The same logic applies everywhere: every additional element has to earn its place.

2. **Warmth without performance.** Copy and interactions can be personal and inviting, but never effusive. The contact section is an invitation, not a pitch. If a flourish exists to make James seem more enthusiastic, cut it.

3. **Designed-feeling, not designed-looking.** Details reward the visitor who looks closely — asymmetric layouts, considered hover states, careful typographic pairings — but they do not announce themselves. Nothing should read as decorative.

4. **No funnel tactics.** No urgency, no scarcity, no "limited spots", no testimonial pyramids, no big primary CTAs that imply a sales motion. The contact action is one mailto link and a quiet "Let's talk".

5. **Asymmetry over template.** Each section gets its own shape — never the icon-left/text-right card repeated four times. Variation between sections is part of the design, not noise.

6. **Quiet hover states.** Inline links inherit colour and reveal an underline on hover (e.g. the footer Claude credit). CTAs go from `--interactive` to `--interactive-d1`. Nothing pulses, bounces, or otherwise tries to grab attention.

### Planned Additions

- **Projects section** between Now and Story. The audience the page is built for (technical peers, especially staff and principal engineers curious about agentic engineering) wants verifiable evidence, not just claims. The Now section asserts a position on harness engineering; a Projects section will back it up with concrete artefacts — links to tools, posts, repos, or experiments. Until it ships, the page is asserting a position without proof, which is the highest-impact unresolved finding from the most recent design critique.
