Welcome

Below you'll find a summary of what I'm working on now and a timeline of the career behind it. If something here sparks an idea or you'd like to find out more, please get in touch.

Now

Harness engineering and the rise of the software factory.

My current focus is agentic engineering. As we move from writing software ourselves to building the harness within which agents operate, how do we maintain the quality, determinism and scale we've always cared about? This isn't vibe coding. The tools are moving fast but the fundamentals haven't changed.

In practice, that means providing an agent with sufficient context before it starts (architecture decisions, conventions, constraints, etc.) and the sensors necessary to verify the output afterwards. This involves a mix of deterministic computational tools like linters and type checkers, and inferential tools that can reason about what the agent produced.

The goal is the dark factory: agents doing the work with minimal intervention within a harness designed to keep quality high throughout. Human judgment still matters but it belongs in the harness, not the loop.

My Story

30+ years of software craftsmanship.

I built my first website in 1995. There was no Javascript, no CSS, just HTML 2.0 and Netscape Navigator. I was lucky enough to find myself in the middle of the dot-com boom and the beginning of a long career in software engineering. In the intervening years I was introduced to agile development and honed my craft in a variety of industries such as telecoms, banking, publishing, food, media and travel. A lot has changed, I've seen many technologies come and go, but the fundamentals have remained the same. The values and principles that underpin high quality, sustainable product development are the same as they ever were: simplicity, fast feedback, good communication, empathy, respect and courage.

  1. 1995

    First website.

    While studying at University College, London, I was given access to my own webspace and built my earliest interactive sites using CGI scripts in Perl.

  2. 1998

    Internet bank.

    I worked on one of the first internet banks, a transformational project for The Woolwich. Notable enough to be referenced by Andy Grove, then CEO of Intel, in a speech to the Confederation of British Industry in September 1999.

  3. 2003, ThoughtWorks

    Agile & XP.

    I was introduced to Extreme Programming on one of the largest agile projects of its time. The experience changed how I think about software engineering and set the direction for everything that followed.

  4. 2016

    Management & leadership.

    After years honing my craft across a variety of industries, picking up domain-driven design, lean methodologies, and ongoing work with Scala and JavaScript, I started asking a different question: how do I make the people around me more effective? It led me into management.

  5. 2017

    300 people.

    My role expanded quickly: 300 people in six disciplines across six countries. At that scale, with distributed teams, my focus shifted from the technology and getting the best out of our people became the priority.

  6. 2025

    Hands-on again.

    Management was never meant to be permanent. Returning to hands-on software engineering, I brought my leadership experience with me and found the combination gave me a richer, more grounded approach to delivering value.

  7. 2026

    Agentic engineering.

    The next shift is already underway. Moving from building software to building the harness within which agents build it, with the same focus on quality and sustainability that has always mattered. The goal is the dark factory: high output, high quality, minimal intervention.
