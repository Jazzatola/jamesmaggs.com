Welcome

a principal engineer based in London. I've spent a long career building software across many industries, with many different people, and I love to pass on what I've learned.

Now

Harness engineering and the rise of the software factory.

My current focus is agentic engineering. As we shift from writing software ourselves to building the harness within which agents operate, how do we hold on to the quality, determinism and scale we've always cared about? This isn't vibe coding. The tools are moving fast but the fundamentals haven't changed.

In practice, that means providing an agent with sufficient context before it starts and the sensors necessary to verify the output. This involves a mix of deterministic computational tools such as linters and type checkers, and inferential tools that can reason about what the agent produced.

The goal is the dark factory: agents doing the work with minimal intervention within a harness designed to keep quality high throughout. Human judgment still matters, but it belongs in the harness, not the loop.

My Story

30+ years of software craftsmanship.

I built my first website in 1995. HTML 2.0, no CSS or JavaScript. Since then I've honed my craft across telecoms, banking, publishing, food, media and travel. Technology keeps changing. The values and principles that underpin high quality, sustainable product development are the same as they ever were: simplicity, fast feedback, good communication, empathy, respect and courage.

  1. 1995

    First website.

    At University College London I was given access to my own webspace and built my first interactive sites using CGI scripts in Perl.

  2. 1998

    Internet bank.

    I worked on one of the first internet banks, a transformational project for The Woolwich. Notable enough that Andy Grove, then CEO of Intel, referenced it in a speech to the CBI in September 1999.

  3. 2003, ThoughtWorks

    Agile & XP.

    I was introduced to Extreme Programming on one of the largest agile projects of its time. The experience changed how I think about software engineering and set the direction for everything that followed.

  4. 2016

    Management & leadership.

    After years honing my craft across a variety of industries, I started asking a different question: how do I make the people around me more effective? It led me into my first management role.

  5. 2017

    300 people.

    My role expanded quickly: 300 people in six disciplines across six countries. At that scale my focus shifted from the technology to the team.

  6. 2025

    Hands-on again.

    Management was never meant to be permanent. Returning to hands-on engineering, I brought that leadership experience with me, complementing my technical expertise.

  7. 2026

    Agentic engineering.

    The next shift is already underway. Building the agent harness with the same focus on quality and sustainability that has always mattered. The goal is the dark factory: high output, high quality, minimal intervention.
